FAR, ground coverage
and setbacks explained.

The three numbers that decide how much you can build on a plot, with worked examples for Delhi NCR. Placeholder article for layout review.

M
Ar. [Principal's Name] · Principal Architect & Auditor

Before a single line is drawn, three numbers from the building bye-laws decide the size and shape of what you can build: floor area ratio, ground coverage and setbacks. Understanding them turns a confusing sanction process into simple arithmetic. (placeholder copy)

1. Floor area ratio (FAR)

FAR is the total built-up area you may construct, expressed as a multiple of the plot area. A 300 sq yd plot with an FAR of 2.0 allows a total of 600 sq yd of built-up area across all floors, subject to the other limits below.

Worked examplePlot 300 sq yd × FAR 2.0 = 600 sq yd permissible built-up area. Spread over three floors, that is roughly 200 sq yd per floor before ground coverage is applied.

2. Ground coverage

Ground coverage caps the footprint of the building on the plot. If coverage is limited to 60 percent, the largest floor plate on a 300 sq yd plot is 180 sq yd, regardless of how much FAR remains.

3. Setbacks

Setbacks are the minimum open distances between the building and each plot boundary. They are fixed by plot size and road width, and they often reduce the usable footprint below the ground coverage limit.

  • Front setback: usually the largest, measured from the road boundary.
  • Rear setback: required for light and ventilation to the rear rooms.
  • Side setbacks: may be waived on smaller plots in some zones.
The number that actually limits most houses is not FAR. It is the footprint left after setbacks.

How we use these numbers

Every MV Design Studio project begins with a feasibility note that runs these three calculations for the plot, so the brief is set against what can really be built. As auditors, we run the same check on drawings prepared by others before a sanction file is submitted.

  1. Confirm the plot area from the registered documents, not the site measurement.
  2. Identify the zone, road width and applicable bye-law table.
  3. Calculate FAR, coverage and setbacks, then test them against the brief.
